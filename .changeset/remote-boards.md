A board can keep no log of its own: `board({ remote, watch })` reads and writes another board, which runs its watchers' tails and sends it what matches. So every project on a team can share one board, with no store, scheduler, or blob in each, and still run its watchers in its own code. A board registers its watchers with its remote when someone posts to its `/watchers`, whenever a delivery shows the remote's copy is stale, and at startup when it knows its URL. `vercelProject()` without a `project` matches every project on the board's team, with the access its `can` gives. A registered watcher sees only what its registrant may read, under the remote's access rules at each match; registering needs `read` somewhere and an `https` URL, deliveries go only to public addresses, and a board refuses a delivery that doesn't name it as the registration's owner. A board with a remote registers its own URL (`url`, or on Vercel its production domain), never a request's Host, and its posts' derived IDs are `remote:<principal>:…`, which the remote takes only from that principal.
